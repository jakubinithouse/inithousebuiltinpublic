# What is Here We Ask by Inithouse? A free browser conversation card game: 1,000+ questions, Solo/Couple/Group modes, FAQ

*Posted 2026-09-29*

[Here We Ask](https://hereweask.com) is a free browser-based conversation card game with 1,000+ curated questions across themed decks for couples, friends and families. No download or account needed. Open the site, pick a deck, start talking.

This post answers the questions we get most often: what the game covers, who it works for, how each mode plays, and how it compares to a physical card deck.

## What does Here We Ask do?

Here We Ask puts conversation prompts on your screen, one at a time, organized into themed decks. You pick a deck, draw a card, read the question out loud, and talk about it. Then draw another.

The question library has 1,000+ prompts spread across dozens of decks. Some are light (icebreakers, party starters). Some go deep (values, vulnerability, gratitude). Some are spicy. The range matters because the same group of people might want a different kind of conversation on a Tuesday night versus a Saturday pregame.

New questions are added on a rolling basis. There is also a daily question feature for people who want a single prompt each morning.

## Who is it for?

Three main groups show up:

**Couples** looking for date night prompts, relationship check-ins, or something to do over dinner that is not scrolling. Decks like Closer, The Repair Kit and Heat cover different tones.

**Friends and groups** using it as a party opener, a road trip game, or an alternative to the "so what's new" loop. Decks like Hot Takes, Confessions, 3AM Thoughts and Party are built for groups.

**Solo users** doing reflection or journaling. Solo mode serves one question at a time for self-discovery. Decks like Gratitude and Mirror Mode work well alone.

## How do the three modes work?

| Mode | Players | How it works | Good for |
|------|---------|-------------|----------|
| Solo | 1 | One question at a time, self-paced | Journaling, reflection, waiting rooms |
| Couple | 2 | Turn-based, each person answers before drawing the next card | Date nights, relationship check-ins, long drives |
| Group | 3+ | One question displayed for the whole group, free discussion or round-robin | Parties, dinners, game nights, sleepovers |

All three modes use the same question library. The difference is pacing and turn structure, not content.

## How is this different from a physical card deck?

Physical decks like We're Not Really Strangers have their place. They look good on a table, they make a nice gift, and the tactile experience adds something.

Here We Ask trades that for a few things physical decks cannot do:

**Variety.** A boxed set has 150 to 300 cards. Here We Ask has 1,000+ and keeps growing. You will not cycle through the same prompts in a month of weekly play.

**Zero cost, zero setup.** No purchase, no shipping, no "I forgot the cards at home." Open a browser, pick a deck.

**Remote play.** Two people on a video call can both see the same question. A physical deck needs one person to read the card out loud while the other waits.

**Offline support.** Here We Ask is a PWA (Progressive Web App). Once loaded, it works without an internet connection. Pull it up on a camping trip or a flight.

**No account.** No email, no signup, no profile. Just open and play.

A physical deck wins on ambiance and giftability. A browser game wins on depth, accessibility and spontaneity. They are not competitors so much as different formats for the same activity.

## Where does the data go?

Here We Ask does not require an account, so there is no user profile or login data. Questions are served from the app itself. The PWA caches content locally for offline use.

## Build details

Here We Ask is built as a Progressive Web App. It works in any modern browser on phone, tablet or desktop. The offline capability means the full question library is available after the first load, with no server round-trips needed during play.

The game is part of the [Inithouse](https://inithouse.com) product studio. We build consumer tools and ship them fast. Here We Ask came from a simple observation: good conversations need good prompts, and most people do not carry a card deck in their pocket. A phone browser is always there.
