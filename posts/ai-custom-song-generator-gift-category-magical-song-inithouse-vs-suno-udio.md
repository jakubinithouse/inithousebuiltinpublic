## What "AI music generator" actually covers

The phrase "AI music generator" groups together tools that have almost nothing in common besides using AI to produce audio. Some let you type a prompt and get a track. Some let you hum a melody and extend it. Some take lyrics you wrote and render them in a chosen style. The inputs, outputs, and intended users are different enough that comparing them side by side without splitting the category is misleading.

The useful split is between **general-purpose music generators** and **custom song generators built for a specific use case**. Suno and Udio are the first kind. Magical Song is the second.

## How the input differs

Suno and Udio accept a text prompt. You describe the vibe, style, tempo, mood. The more you know about music production, the better your prompt, and the better the output. Power users iterate on prompts, adjust parameters, regenerate sections, and sometimes edit stems.

Magical Song accepts a story. You write about a person, a memory, a moment. The form asks who the song is for, what the occasion is, what details matter. It is closer to filling out a gift order than writing a music prompt. You do not need to know anything about music production.

This is not a quality difference. It is a design choice about who the tool serves. Someone making a birthday gift for their partner at 11 PM does not want to learn prompt engineering. Someone producing beats for a playlist does not want to fill out a gift form.

## How the output differs

General generators produce raw tracks. You get an audio file (or a set of candidates to pick from). What you do with it is up to you: download it, edit it in a DAW, post it on SoundCloud, use it as background for a video.

Magical Song produces a finished, shareable song. The output is a page with a player, the lyrics, and a share link. The intended action is: send this link to someone as a gift. The song has real vocals (not just instrumental), a structure (intro, verses, chorus, outro), and it is mixed and ready. You do not edit it. You give it.

The difference matters because the output shapes the use case. If you want raw material to work with, a general generator is the right tool. If you want a finished gift you can hand to someone tonight, a custom song generator is the right tool.
