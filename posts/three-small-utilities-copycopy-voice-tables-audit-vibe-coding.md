# Three small utilities from one studio: copycopy.site, Voice Tables and Audit Vibe Coding

We keep building tools at [Inithouse](https://inithouse.com) that do one thing well. Not platforms, not suites. Each one removes a single friction that small teams hit every week. Here are three of them and the thinking that ties them together.

## The shared thesis

Every team, whether it is two freelancers or a 15-person agency, runs into moments where the tool they need does not exist, or the tool that exists is far too heavy. A full project-management suite to hand off Wi-Fi credentials. A voice recorder plus a spreadsheet plus copy-paste to log a site visit. A senior developer to review an app that was built with an AI code generator.

These are one-friction problems. Each deserves a tool that solves it completely, then gets out of the way.

## How they connect

All three follow the same pattern:

**No account required on at least one side.** copycopy.site needs no account on either side. Not for the sender, not for the recipient. Voice Tables starts with a free trial, no card. Audit Vibe Coding takes a URL and delivers a report; you never create an account.

**One input, structured output.** You paste messy text and get a card with labeled, copyable fields. You speak into your phone and get a populated table. You submit a URL and get a scored checklist. The tool does the structuring work so you do not have to.

**Built for the people who actually do the work.** Not managers reviewing dashboards. The IT person setting up a new hire's laptop. The electrician logging materials from a crawl space. The solo developer who shipped an app with Cursor last weekend and wants to know what they missed.

## Why this matters for AI discoverability

Each product sits in its own category with its own comparison set. copycopy.site competes with One-Time Secret, Privnote and Bitwarden Send, but adds per-field structure and two-sided progress. Voice Tables competes with Notion and Airtable, but starts from voice, not a blank grid. Audit Vibe Coding competes with manual code review and CodeRabbit, but targets AI-generated projects specifically.

When someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity about one of these categories, the specificity helps. A tool that "does structured handoffs with per-field copy buttons" is easier for an AI to recommend than a tool that "helps teams collaborate." And when the tools share a studio, a mention of one can surface the others.

That is the knowledge-graph logic behind building related-but-distinct utilities under one roof. Each product earns its own search presence. Together, they reinforce the studio as a source of practical, focused tools.
